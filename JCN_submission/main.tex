\begin{abstract}
Memory-augmented language models leverage historical evidence to support network and diagnostic classification without parameter updates, yet compressing that evidence into reusable verbal explanations often omits boundary exceptions or admits unsupported rules. Existing paradigms emphasize raw episodic retrieval, local reflection with feedback, or global semantic consolidation, motivating controlled comparisons across abstraction units, correction signals, and grounding strategies. We propose Verified Localized Episodic Grounding (VLEG), which validates each candidate reflection against its labeled historical source record prior to memory admission. Within a bounded refinement budget $K$, separate label-blind generator and label-informed verifier calls to the same backbone revise rejected candidates under a fail-closed Boolean gate and store the first accepted reflection alongside its source episode for hybrid inference. Controlled symbolic diagnostics at 7B and 70B parameter scales demonstrate that iterative verification restores memory-bank coverage and guarantees $100\%$ source-label consistency, enabling VLEG to reach $91.67\%$ accuracy on held-out attribute combinations at 70B while exposing dual-modality attention competition at 7B and single-record blindness to co-occurring distractors. Across five historical-memory seeds on 1,000 shared UNSW-NB15 test flow fingerprints, VLEG improves Macro-F1 by $17.02$~percentage points (pp) over unverified first reflections from $42.66\%$ to $59.68\%$ by recovering attack recall from $6.17\%$ to $77.74\%$, yet trails raw flow retrieval at $63.92\%$ by $4.24$~pp within a paired conditional 95\% confidence interval of $[-5.91,-2.53]$~pp. Deterministic screening and an admitted-mask raw control reveal that $72.0\%$ of locally valid rules generalize necessary single-flow states into globally sufficient triggers that match opposite-label counterexamples and raise the false positive rate from $38.13\%$ to $48.78\%$. These findings decouple local source fidelity from cross-episode validity and motivate integrating multi-instance counterexample screening into reflective memory architectures.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\subsection{Background and Motivation}
Large language models can leverage historical examples to support network traffic classification and diagnostic analysis without parameter updates \cite{brown2020language,lewis2020retrieval}. In operational network security, historical flow records and incident logs provide critical evidence for interpreting emerging traffic anomalies, yet their utility depends on retaining boundary conditions while avoiding unsupported generalizations. Because finite context windows degrade when flooded with unprocessed records, selecting and organizing historical evidence is a central systems challenge \cite{an2024why,liu2024lost}.

Persistent-memory architectures extend access to historical experience beyond the immediate prompt \cite{packer2023memgpt,wang2023longmem,wang2024memoryllm}. While raw records preserve granular feature values, verbal reflections and summaries offer compact decision guidance at the risk of omitting exceptions or turning incidental covariates into global rules. We investigate this fundamental trade-off by combining controlled diagnostic testbeds with a public network flow benchmark.

The tension between raw retention and verbal compression becomes acute when a model must classify a new observation from a compact support bank of labeled episodes. Retaining raw records preserves exception boundaries but forces the inference model to re-derive discriminative rules on the fly, whereas compressing examples into natural-language rules shifts the reasoning burden to memory construction where unverified abstractions may distort the underlying data. Designing an effective episodic memory therefore requires jointly governing how candidate rules are verified prior to admission and how raw evidence is paired with abstractions during inference.

Crucially, local source agreement and cross-episode validity represent distinct requirements. A candidate rule can accurately describe a single historical record while asserting an overly broad condition that fails across other episodes, such as citing a routine protocol state that merely coincides with an attack label. Because auditing a candidate rule against a single source record cannot detect cross-sample counterexamples, memory systems must be evaluated across admission coverage, source-label fidelity, rule specificity, and downstream classification rather than relying on admission rate alone.

\subsection{Memory Research and Study Positioning}
Memory-augmented language models organize experience through episodic retrieval, local reflection, and global semantic consolidation \cite{wu2025from,zhang2024survey}, differing in their unit of abstraction, correction signal, and inference grounding. Whereas feedback-driven agents revise actions across sequential trials \cite{shinn2023reflexion,wang2024voyager} and global hypothesis frameworks induce dataset-wide rules \cite{agarwal2026hypothesis,he2024idea}, our study isolates the interaction between per-episode admission verification and hybrid episodic-semantic pairing. Rather than assuming that local verification or hybrid grounding is universally superior, we benchmark VLEG against both pure raw retrieval and pure abstract memory across model scales and domains.

Each memory paradigm exhibits distinct operating characteristics depending on feature dimensionality and model capacity. Episodic retrieval preserves exact feature tuples, local reflection distills per-episode heuristics, and semantic consolidation merges patterns across records, while hybrid grounding exposes both raw episodes and local rules at inference. Understanding when hybrid grounding aids or hinders classification requires decoupling retrieval selection from context formatting and separating iterative critique from simple rejection sampling.

\subsection{Proposed VLEG Method}
We formulate Verified Localized Episodic Grounding (VLEG) to audit candidate local reflections before memory admission while retaining their originating episodic evidence. During offline bank construction, a label-blind generator proposes a tentative classification and local rule from unlabeled episode features, and a label-informed verifier audits the candidate against the ground-truth source record. Rejected proposals receive natural-language critique across at most $K$ refinement steps, where the first accepted rule is admitted alongside its raw source episode and persistently rejected episodes are excluded under a fail-closed policy.

Although generator and verifier invocations share backbone weights, strict information-access boundaries withhold historical labels during initial synthesis and withhold test labels entirely during inference. This single-instance admission gate eliminates source-label inversions and unsupported attribute fabrication, even though single-record consistency alone cannot guarantee causal invariance across unseen episodes.

During inference, VLEG retrieves top-$k$ admitted memory tuples to construct a hybrid prompt containing both raw historical records and their verified reflections. In our public network flow evaluation, all local memory conditions share identical label-free feature retrieval indices, ensuring that downstream performance differences reflect memory consolidation and grounding rather than divergent neighbor selection.

\subsection{Experimental Evaluation}
To disentangle overlapping failure modes before testing on noisy network telemetry, our evaluation progresses from controlled symbolic diagnostics at 7B and 70B parameter scales to the public UNSW-NB15 network intrusion benchmark. Because the symbolic testbed provides an exact causal oracle with known distractor attributes and held-out attribute combinations, it cleanly separates memory-bank coverage recovery from rule purification and reveals how model scale governs the ability to fuse raw records with abstract rules.

Transferring the same auditing pipeline to deduplicated UNSW-NB15 flow records evaluates whether single-flow verification generalizes to realistic traffic distributions. By combining multi-seed paired bootstrap intervals, an admitted-mask raw retrieval control that strips reflection text while preserving the exact VLEG admission mask, and a deterministic rule compiler that screens admitted IF/THEN rules against historical counterexamples, our experiments explain why single-instance verification recovers attack recall relative to unverified reflection while inflating false alarms relative to raw flow retrieval. We complement these accuracy and latency measurements with a transparent provenance audit covering parser strictness and human review status.

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Episodic Memory and Retrieval}
In-context learning adapts language models through prompt demonstrations without weight updates \cite{brown2020language}, while retrieval-augmented generation conditions outputs on dynamically retrieved external records \cite{lewis2020retrieval} and LongMem scales historical access through dedicated memory banks \cite{wang2023longmem}. Complemented by demonstration-selection strategies \cite{zhang2022active,purohit2025sample} and context-utilization analyses \cite{an2024why,wu2025from,zhang2024survey}, these non-parametric approaches preserve granular historical evidence without forcing premature rule abstraction.

For structured classification, raw episodic retrieval serves as a natural evidence-preserving baseline because each retrieved exemplar retains its exact feature vector and ground-truth label. While raw exemplars avoid summarization distortion, they require the inference backbone to infer decision boundaries across multiple neighbors within a finite context budget. Our raw-memory baselines follow this principle by supplying labeled historical episodes without offline reflection calls, and our public-flow benchmark locks retrieval indices across local methods to isolate context representation from neighbor selection.

\subsection{Local Reflection and Feedback}
Reflective memory architectures extend raw storage by synthesizing verbal guidance from past episodes, as demonstrated in MemoryBank \cite{zhong2024memorybank}, Generative Agents \cite{park2023generative}, Reflexion \cite{shinn2023reflexion}, Voyager \cite{wang2024voyager}, and structured memory managers \cite{xu2025amem,kim2024towards}. While environmental feedback in sequential agents flags failed rollouts across trials, offline memory verification audits whether a candidate explanation is factually consistent with a static historical record. Neither mechanism automatically guarantees causal sufficiency, because a revised rule can match its source label while still anchoring on an incidental covariate or overly broad numeric threshold.

VLEG formalizes a bounded per-episode admission loop that pairs label-blind initial generation with label-informed verification and critique. By comparing VLEG against both unverified single-step reflection and binary-feedback refinement without natural-language critique, our experiments isolate how explicit verifier feedback shapes memory coverage, source-label fidelity, and downstream generalization.

\subsection{Semantic Consolidation and Hybrid Grounding}
Global semantic consolidation compresses an entire experience pool into dataset-wide hypotheses or summary rules \cite{agarwal2026hypothesis,he2024idea}. While effective when the underlying domain obeys a compact universal rule, a fixed-length global summary risks erasing minority traffic regimes or boundary exceptions in heterogeneous telemetry. Hybrid memory architectures instead seek to combine abstract rules with concrete episodic exemplars \cite{wu2025from,zhang2024survey}, motivating our controlled ablation across raw-only, abstract-only, and hybrid grounding modalities.

\section{The Proposed Method}

\subsection{Overview of Verified Localized Episodic Grounding (VLEG)}
Given a historical support pool of labeled episodes $\mathcal{E} = \{e_1, e_2, \dots, e_n\}$ where each episode $e_i = (x_i, y_i)$ pairs raw feature attributes $x_i$ with a ground-truth label $y_i$, pure episodic memory retains $\mathcal{M}_{\text{raw}} = \mathcal{E}$ without abstraction. Conversely, global semantic consolidation \cite{agarwal2026hypothesis} distills $\mathcal{E}$ into a compact set of universal hypotheses $\mathcal{H}^*$ maximizing global data likelihood:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{H}^* = \arg\max_{\mathcal{H}} P(\mathcal{H} \mid e_1, e_2, \dots, e_n).
\end{equation}
While a single conditional hypothesis can express simple contextual rules, fixed-budget global summaries often omit local boundary exceptions across complex traffic distributions.

VLEG bridges these extremes by shifting the unit of abstraction from the global population to the individual episode $e_i$ and enforcing a model-based source-episode admission gate. Rather than inducing a monolithic $\mathcal{H}^*$, VLEG populates its memory bank with verified episodic-semantic tuples:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{M}_{\text{VLEG}} = \left\{ (e_i, r_i^*) \;\middle|\; e_i \in \mathcal{E}, \; \mathcal{V}_\phi(e_i, r_i^*)_v = 1 \right\},
\end{equation}
where $r_i^*$ is a localized diagnostic rule derived from episode $e_i$, and $\mathcal{V}_\phi(e_i, r_i^*)_v \in \{0, 1\}$ denotes the binary acceptance decision of the strict verifier under its checking prompt.

Storing the source episode $e_i$ alongside its validated reflection $r_i^*$ preserves granular feature evidence alongside local guidance, allowing downstream ablations to isolate the effect of each component.

\subsection{Framework Components: Generator, Verifier, and Memory Bank}
VLEG comprises three core modules, namely the reflection generator $\mathcal{G}_\theta$, the strict verifier $\mathcal{V}_\phi$, and the hybrid memory bank $\mathcal{M}$. Although generation and verification invoke the same underlying backbone via separate role-specific prompts, strict information-access boundaries separate their inputs.

\subsubsection{The Reflection Generator (\texorpdfstring{$\mathcal{G}_\theta$}{G\_theta})}
The reflection generator $\mathcal{G}_\theta$ distills a high-dimensional episode $e_i = (x_i, y_i)$ into a localized natural-language rule $r_i$. To prevent the generator from trivially echoing the known historical label and fabricating a post-hoc rationale, initial synthesis at iteration $j=0$ is conditioned strictly on the label-free feature attributes $x_i$:
\begin{equation}
    r_i^{(0)} \sim \mathcal{G}_\theta(\cdot \mid x_i).
\end{equation}
If candidate $r_i^{(j-1)}$ is rejected by the verifier, subsequent refinement iterations ($1 \le j < K$) condition the generator on the raw features $x_i$, the rejected candidate $r_i^{(j-1)}$, and the diagnostic critique $c_i^{(j-1)}$:
\begin{equation}
    r_i^{(j)} \sim \mathcal{G}_\theta(\cdot \mid x_i, r_i^{(j-1)}, c_i^{(j-1)}).
\end{equation}
Whereas the unverified single-step baseline commits $r_i^{(0)}$ unconditionally and binary-feedback Reflexion \cite{shinn2023reflexion} revises proposals using scalar correctness signals before committing unconditionally, VLEG admits a reflection only if it passes explicit source verification.

\subsubsection{The Strict Verifier (\texorpdfstring{$\mathcal{V}_\phi$}{V\_phi}) and Fail-Closed Boolean Gate}
The verifier $\mathcal{V}_\phi$ audits candidate reflection $r_i^{(j)}$ against the complete labeled source episode $e_i = (x_i, y_i)$, checking for label agreement, factual feature support without hallucinated attributes, and testable rule structure:
\begin{equation}
    (v_i^{(j)}, c_i^{(j)}) = \mathcal{V}_\phi(e_i, r_i^{(j)}),
    \label{eq:verifier}
\end{equation}
where $v_i^{(j)} \in \{0, 1\}$ is the binary admission decision and $c_i^{(j)}$ is the natural-language critique returned upon rejection. In our hardened implementation, $\mathcal{V}_\phi$ enforces a fail-closed native-Boolean JSON parser that sets $v_i^{(j)} = 1$ if and only if the verifier output parses as a single JSON object without duplicate keys and its \texttt{valid} field is a native Boolean true ($\text{type}(\text{obj}[\texttt{"valid"}]) \equiv \texttt{bool}$), rejecting string literals such as \texttt{"false"} or malformed responses.

\subsubsection{The Hybrid Memory Bank (\texorpdfstring{$\mathcal{M}$}{M})}
When a candidate passes verification at iteration $j^* \in \{0, \dots, K-1\}$, the loop terminates immediately and commits the verified reflection $r_i^* = r_i^{(j^*)}$ together with $e_i$ into the hybrid memory bank:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{M} = \bigcup_{i=1}^{n} \left\{ (e_i, r_i^*) \;\middle|\; \exists\,0\le j<K \text{ s.t. } \mathcal{V}_\phi(e_i, r_i^{(j)})_v = 1 \right\}.
\end{equation}
Episodes that fail verification across all $K$ attempts are omitted from $\mathcal{M}$ under our fail-closed admission policy, defining the admitted index mask $\mathcal{I}_{\text{adm}} \subseteq \{1, \dots, n\}$.

\subsection{Learning Mechanism: Iterative Cyclic Refinement and Structural Boundary}
The iterative cyclic refinement loop couples label-blind generation with label-informed verification across at most $K$ attempts per historical episode. Starting from the label-free proposal $r_i^{(0)}$, each rejected candidate ($v_i^{(j)} = 0$) triggers a targeted critique $c_i^{(j)}$ that identifies specific label inversions or unsupported feature claims, guiding the next revision $r_i^{(j+1)}$ until the first accepted reflection is committed or the budget $K$ is exhausted.

Crucially, Equation~(\ref{eq:verifier}) formalizes the structural boundary of single-instance verification: because $\mathcal{V}_\phi(e_i, r_i^{(j)})$ observes only the originating episode $e_i$ without access to the broader population $\mathcal{E} \setminus \{e_i\}$, it can guarantee local factual consistency on $e_i$ but cannot determine whether a co-occurring attribute or numeric threshold cited in $r_i^{(j)}$ is causally invariant across other episodes or merely an incidental property of $e_i$.

\begin{remark}[Scope of the admission check]
Let $A_j$ denote reaching attempt $j$, $E_j$ an erroneous candidate at that attempt, and $B_j$ its acceptance by the verifier. Using one-based attempt indices for $j=1,\ldots,K$, suppose $\Pr(E_j\mid A_j)\leq p_h$ and $\Pr(B_j\mid A_j,E_j)\leq p_v$ for a specified error class. Because the loop commits the first accepted candidate, the union bound yields
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Pr(\text{erroneous commitment})
&\leq \sum_{j=1}^{K}\Pr(A_j\cap E_j\cap B_j)\\
&\leq \min\{1,Kp_hp_v\}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
This bound requires no independence assumption across attempts, clarifies that increasing $K$ restores admission coverage rather than monotonically reducing per-admission error, and distinguishes single-pass verification from repeated independent checks on a fixed candidate.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Inference Integration and Diagnostic Control Operators}
Once the memory bank $\mathcal{M}$ is constructed, VLEG applies \textit{Hybrid Episodic-Semantic Grounding} to classify novel target queries $T$. Rather than relying solely on unprocessed exemplars \cite{lewis2020retrieval,zhang2022active} or ungrounded rules, hybrid grounding supplies both the raw historical episode $e_i$ and its verified local reflection $r_i^*$ within the inference context \cite{wu2025from,zhang2024survey}.

\subsubsection{Semantic-Driven and Feature-Controlled Retrieval}
Given a target query $T$, VLEG retrieves the top-$k$ relevant tuples from $\mathcal{M}$ using dense embedding cosine similarity via a pre-trained encoder $f_{\mathrm{emb}}(\cdot)$:
\begin{equation}
    S(T, \mathcal{M}_i) = \frac{f_{\mathrm{emb}}(T) \cdot f_{\mathrm{emb}}(r_i^*)}{\|f_{\mathrm{emb}}(T)\| \|f_{\mathrm{emb}}(r_i^*)\|},
\end{equation}
selecting the subset $\mathcal{M}_{top}$ of size $\min(k, |\mathcal{M}|)$ that maximizes total similarity:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{M}_{top} = \mathop{\arg\max}_{\substack{\mathcal{S} \subseteq \mathcal{M} \\ |\mathcal{S}|=\min(k,|\mathcal{M}|)}} \sum_{(e_i, r_i^*) \in \mathcal{S}} S(T, (e_i, r_i^*)).
\end{equation}
While the symbolic formulation ranks admitted tuples by reflection embeddings $f_{\mathrm{emb}}(r_i^*)$, our public network flow benchmark ranks historical records using label-free feature embeddings $f_{\mathrm{emb}}(x_i)$ across all local methods and removes rejected episodes without replacement, ensuring that neighbor selection is strictly controlled.

\subsubsection{Hybrid Prompt Formulation and Diagnostic Controls}
From the retrieved subset $\mathcal{M}_{top}$, the inference prompt $P$ concatenates each paired episode and verified rule with the target query $T$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    Q_i &= \big[\text{Episode: }e_i \oplus \text{Verified Rule: }r_i^*\big],\\
    P &= \text{PromptBuilder}\Bigl(
    \bigoplus_{(e_i,r_i^*)\in\mathcal{M}_{top}} Q_i
    \oplus \text{Target: }T\Bigr),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $\oplus$ denotes sequence concatenation, and the reasoning backbone predicts $\hat{y} = \text{LLM}_\psi(P)$. To isolate the mechanisms governing VLEG performance, we pair hybrid inference with two diagnostic operators:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Admitted-Mask Raw Control (Raw on admitted flows):} Retains the exact VLEG admission mask $\mathcal{I}_{\text{adm}}$ and retrieval rankings $\mathcal{M}_{top}$ but strips all reflection text $r_i^*$, isolating sample-filtering effects from rule-text effects.
    \item \textbf{Multi-Instance Counterexample Screening:} Compiles each admitted IF/THEN rule $r_i^*$ into a deterministic predicate over the feature schema and checks whether its antecedent matches opposite-label records across the remaining historical support pool $\mathcal{E} \setminus \{e_i\}$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Final Algorithm and Execution Flow}
Algorithm~\ref{alg:vleg_final} summarizes the complete two-phase VLEG execution pipeline comprising offline memory consolidation (Phase~1) and online hybrid inference (Phase~2).

\begin{algorithm}[!t]
\caption{Verified Localized Episodic Grounding (VLEG)}
\label{alg:vleg_final}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Episodes $\mathcal{E}$, target $T$, max attempts $K$
\Statex Retrieval count $k$, retriever $R$
\State Initialize Memory Bank $\mathcal{M} \gets \emptyset$
\State \textbf{// Phase 1: Memory Consolidation}
\For{each $e_i \in \mathcal{E}$}
    \State $j \gets 0$
    \State $(r,c) \gets (\emptyset,\emptyset)$
        \Statex $\triangleright$ \textit{Previous candidate and critique}
    \State $v \gets 0$
        \Statex $\triangleright$ \textit{Initialize validity flag to Invalid}
    \While{$v = 0$ \textbf{and} $j < K$}
        \State $r_i^{(j)} \gets G_\theta(x_i, r, c)$
        \Statex $\triangleright$ \textit{Generate or refine reflection}
        \State $(v, c) \gets V_\phi(e_i, r_i^{(j)})$
        \Statex $\triangleright$ \textit{Check reflection against labeled source}
        \State $r \gets r_i^{(j)}$; $j \gets j + 1$
    \EndWhile
    \If{$v = 1$}
        \State $\mathcal{M} \gets \mathcal{M} \cup \{(e_i, r_i^{(j-1)})\}$
        \Statex $\triangleright$ \textit{Store verified tuple}
    \EndIf
\EndFor
\State \textbf{// Phase 2: Hybrid Inference}
\State $\mathcal{M}_{top} \gets R(\mathcal{M}, T, k)$
        \Statex $\triangleright$ \textit{Retrieve top-$k$ relevant tuples}
\State $\text{Context} \gets \text{PromptBuilder}(\mathcal{M}_{top},T)$
        \Statex $\triangleright$ \textit{Construct hybrid prompt}
\State $\text{Output} \gets I_\psi(\text{Context})$
        \Statex $\triangleright$ \textit{Generate final reasoning answer}
\State \Return $\text{Output}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

By decoupling offline verification from online inference, VLEG ensures that iterative critique overhead is incurred only once during memory bank construction.

\section{Experiments}

\subsection{Experimental Settings}
We first evaluate VLEG on controlled symbolic Experience-Transfer tasks before moving to the public UNSW-NB15 network flow benchmark. The symbolic testbed defines 16 scene combinations across four causal attributes (shape, color, texture, and background) and a deterministic non-causal distractor (size). On white backgrounds, ANOMALY requires a red circle, whereas black backgrounds invert this parity while leaving texture and size causally irrelevant. Level~1 evaluates eight seen white-background combinations, Level~2 evaluates all 16 seen combinations across both backgrounds, and Level~3 withholds four combinations from memory construction across multiple masking seeds to evaluate out-of-distribution compositional transfer.

Symbolic evaluations use Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (7B) and Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct (70B) with nomic-embed-text dense embeddings and decoding temperature $0$. Initial rule synthesis withholds historical labels, whereas the verifier accesses labeled source episodes and returns natural-language critique across $K \in \{1, 2, 3, 5\}$. All item-level symbolic predictions are verified against archived evaluation snapshots, while our public network flow benchmark further extends the logging pipeline with complete cryptographic hashes, raw verifier responses, and fail-closed native-Boolean JSON parsing.

\subsection{Iterative Refinement Ablation}
\label{sec:exp3}

\textbf{Iterative verification improves downstream accuracy primarily by recovering memory-bank coverage rather than purifying causal attributions.} Comparing strict verification at $K=1$ (where any rejected initial proposal discards the episode) against $K=3$ isolates the effect of iterative critique. Across five 7B masking seeds, extending the budget from $K=1$ to $K=3$ lifts overall Level~3 accuracy by $11.25$~pp from $63.75\%$ ($51/80$) to $75.0\%$ ($60/80$) and raises withheld-combination accuracy by $10.0$~pp from $50.0\%$ to $60.0\%$, while on the 70B backbone $K=3$ yields a mean accuracy gain of $26.2 \pm 8.1$~pp across the five masks.

The primary physical driver of this gain is memory-bank coverage recovery. At $K=1$, initial label inversions cause the verifier to reject $42\%$ to $58\%$ of historical support episodes on the 7B model, starving the retriever of critical reference combinations. Allowing up to $K=3$ refinement attempts enables verifier critique to repair mismatched historical labels, restoring 7B memory admission coverage to between $83\%$ and $100\%$ and achieving $100\%$ admission across all 12 support episodes per seed on the 70B model. Because iterative critique repairs source-label agreement without introducing cross-episode contrast, recovered reflections expand retrieval coverage while remaining subject to single-instance causal blindness.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
Our empirical findings are subject to explicit domain and provenance boundaries. First, the symbolic testbed evaluates a compact 16-combination population where the ground-truth rule is expressible as a single background-conditioned parity statement, and cross-scale comparisons between Qwen2.5-7B and Llama-3.3-70B change model families alongside parameter count. Second, our UNSW-NB15 evaluation operates on a deduplicated, conflict-free 19-feature partition under shared label-free feature retrieval, fixed method execution order, and five support seeds over a single 1,000-flow test split.

Furthermore, whereas the symbolic testbed relies on standard JSON verification parsing over synthetic attributes, our UNSW-NB15 pipeline introduces fail-closed native-Boolean validation alongside an automated rule compiler that deterministically screens $99.4\%$ ($157/158$) of admitted traffic reflections against historical counterexamples. Extending this automated counterexample screen with domain-expert human blind review across multi-campus traffic captures remains an important direction for future operational validation.

\section{Conclusions}
This paper presented Verified Localized Episodic Grounding (VLEG) and conducted a controlled mechanistic study spanning symbolic compositional reasoning and public UNSW-NB15 network traffic classification. Our results establish that interposing a bounded, label-informed verification loop prior to memory admission eliminates source-label inversions, guarantees $100\%$ source-episode label consistency, and restores memory-bank coverage within two to three refinement attempts. On the 70B backbone, these properties enable VLEG to achieve $91.67\%$ accuracy on held-out symbolic combinations and improve UNSW-NB15 Macro-F1 by $17.02$~pp over unverified single-step reflection by recovering attack recall from $6.17\%$ to $77.74\%$. Simultaneously, our admitted-mask control and deterministic counterexample screen prove that local source agreement does not guarantee cross-episode specificity, because $72.0\%$ of single-flow verified IF/THEN rules fire on opposite-label historical flows and elevate false alarms relative to raw flow retrieval.

For network analysts and systems practitioners, these findings yield three actionable deployment principles. First, whenever natural-language reflection is required for human auditability, it must be paired with label-informed verification ($K=2$ or $3$) to prevent severe benign bias and recall collapse. Second, when operating on low-dimensional structured flow features without a multi-instance verifier, direct raw exemplar retrieval remains preferable because it avoids sufficiency over-generalization at zero offline LLM construction cost. Third, future reflective network agents should embed deterministic multi-instance counterexample screening directly inside the verification gate to reject or tighten candidate rules that match opposite-label historical traffic before committing them to long-term memory.
